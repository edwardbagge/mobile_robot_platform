test_09
Result: SUCCESS
Purpose:
- ROS 2 bridge from /robot_command string messages to ESP32 serial motor commands.

What worked:
- Package built successfully.
- Serial port /dev/ttyUSB0 opened successfully.
- Commands s, f, x, and z were sent from ROS 2 and received by the ESP32.
- ESP32 reported status correctly.
- Forward command produced encoder feedback.
- Motor timeout stop worked.
- Encoder reset worked.

Notes:
- ESP32 reboot text appeared at startup, but this was expected because the ESP32 reset button was pressed physically at the start of the test.
- During forward motion, left ticks were positive and right ticks were negative. This may be normal depending on the encoder sign convention.


test_10
Result: PARTIAL SUCCESS
Purpose:
- ROS 2 bridge from /cmd_vel Twist messages to ESP32 serial motor commands.

What worked:
- Serial port /dev/ttyUSB0 opened successfully.
- /cmd_vel commands were mapped and sent correctly:
  - forward -> f
  - backward -> b
  - left turn -> l
  - right turn -> r
  - stop -> x
- ESP32 received each command and reported the matching motor action.

Issues observed:
- Forward motion looked plausible and both encoder values changed.
- Backward motion was suspicious: left encoder changed significantly, but right encoder stayed almost fixed near -1160.
- Right turn was also suspicious: left encoder changed significantly, but right encoder stayed almost fixed near -2330.

Conclusion:
- The /cmd_vel to serial bridge worked.
- The test did not cleanly validate balanced drivetrain or right encoder behavior across all motions.


test_11
Result: PARTIAL SUCCESS
Purpose:
- Combined /cmd_vel to ESP32 serial bridge plus parsing and publishing encoder feedback to ROS topics.

What worked:
- Serial port /dev/ttyUSB0 opened successfully.
- The node sent the expected motor commands from /cmd_vel input.
- Encoder feedback lines from the ESP32 were parsed successfully.
- Parsed values were published to:
  - /left_encoder_ticks
  - /right_encoder_ticks
- Topic echoes matched the parsed values from the general log.

Issues observed:
- The right encoder showed little or no change during backward motion while the left encoder changed substantially.
- The right encoder also changed very little during right-turn motion while the left encoder changed substantially.

Conclusion:
- ROS integration worked: command bridging, serial parsing, and encoder topic publishing were successful.
- The test does not show clean, correct encoder behavior for both wheels under all commanded motions.


test_12
Result: PARTIAL SUCCESS
Purpose:
- Wheel odometry test using /left_encoder_ticks and /right_encoder_ticks to publish /odom.

What worked:
- The `wheel_odometry_test` node started successfully.
- The node subscribed to:
  - /left_encoder_ticks
  - /right_encoder_ticks
- The node published /odom at the configured rate.
- /odom messages were available after the node started.

Issues observed:
- The first `ros2 topic echo --once /odom` was attempted before /odom was visible and returned a warning that the topic was not published yet.
- Forward motion did not look like straight travel in odometry. Encoder counts diverged strongly during the forward command, and the resulting odom pose already showed substantial turning and lateral displacement.
- Backward motion also did not look like straight travel. The right encoder stayed almost fixed while the left encoder changed significantly, and odom continued to accumulate a curved path.
- Turning commands produced large translation as well as rotation, which suggests the odometry was being driven by unbalanced or incorrect encoder inputs rather than clean in-place turning.
- After the final stop command, encoder counts still changed and the final odom pose also shifted.

Conclusion:
- The test_12 node itself appears functional as a ROS node: it starts, subscribes, and publishes /odom.
- The overall odometry result is not healthy enough to count as a clean pass because the input encoder data appears inconsistent, especially across forward, backward, and right-turn behavior.
- The main problem appears upstream of odometry publication, likely in encoder behavior, encoder sign/direction handling, drivetrain balance, or bridge/firmware processing.


test_13
Result: SUCCESS
Purpose:
- RPLidar bring-up test using the `rplidar_ros` driver and RViz.

What worked:
- The launch file started successfully with serial port `/dev/ttyUSB0`.
- The RPLidar driver started and identified the device successfully.
- The device reported healthy status:
  - RPLidar health status: OK
- The driver entered active scanning mode successfully.
- RViz started successfully during the launch.
- ROS 2 topics expected for lidar operation were present, including:
  - /scan
  - /tf
  - /tf_static
- `ros2 topic echo /scan` showed valid `sensor_msgs/LaserScan` data with:
  - `frame_id: laser`
  - plausible angle and range fields
  - real distance returns mixed with `.inf` values, which is normal for no-return regions
- `ros2 topic hz /scan` measured a stable scan rate around 10.56 Hz, which is consistent with the reported 10.0 Hz operating mode.

Issues observed:
- On shutdown, the lidar node did not terminate within 5 seconds after SIGINT, so launch escalated to SIGTERM before the process exited cleanly.

Conclusion:
- The lidar bring-up was successful.
- The sensor was detected, produced scan data on `/scan`, and operated at the expected rate.
- The only observed issue was slightly untidy shutdown behavior, not runtime functionality.
